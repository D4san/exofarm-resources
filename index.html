01 / THE SCIENTIFIC QUESTION

From farming to an atmospheric signal.

Industrial agriculture alters Earth's nitrogen cycle. Could similar planetary-scale activity leave a recognizable atmospheric signature around another star?

THE IDEA

ExoFarm

Intensive agriculture can enhance emissions of nitrous oxide (N₂O) and ammonia (NH₃). This creates a plausible candidate for a long-lived atmospheric technosignature, not a unique proof of technology.

Concept introduced by Haqq-Misra et al. (2022).

THE CHALLENGE

One emission, different atmospheres

The same surface flux need not produce the same atmospheric abundance. Stellar ultraviolet irradiation and chemical sinks shape how these molecules accumulate, persist and appear in spectra.

Photochemical context: Segura et al. (2005); Huang et al. (2022).

N₂O
Nitrous oxide

A comparatively persistent tracer of nitrogen-cycle perturbation; its atmospheric abundance and vertical profile depend on photochemical losses.

NH₃
Ammonia

Strongly affected by photochemistry and efficient surface sinks. In the modeled M-dwarf environments, it can persist over a wider atmospheric range.

02 / HOW IT WORKS

Following the signal, step by step.

Rather than directly prescribing atmospheric abundances, the study starts with surface emission fluxes and follows their photochemical and observational consequences.

03 / MAIN FINDINGS

What does the signal tell us?

Results from the configurations presented in the conference poster. These are simulated observations, not detections of exoplanetary agriculture.

PHOTOCHEMISTRY

M-dwarf irradiation matters

Stellar irradiation can favor accumulation and vertical persistence of nitrogen-bearing species, especially NH₃.

OBSERVABILITY

N₂O is more accessible

The simulated LIFE configuration offers greater molecular sensitivity than the modeled JWST transmission case.

BAYESIAN EVIDENCE

NH₃ needs longer exposures

Under extreme ExoFarm forcing, moderate evidence for NH₃ appears at 100 hours.

INTERPRETATION

Enrichment is not attribution

Joint N₂O and NH₃ enrichment supports intensified nitrogen cycling, but does not uniquely establish agriculture.
